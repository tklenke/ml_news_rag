Moving a Cozy by trailer is doable, but the setup depends on the model and how complete the aircraft is.

- **Reduce the size first.** For one Cozy III move, the owner removed the wings, propeller, and canard; loading took about two hours. Wings and other parts can be padded and secured separately on the trailer. [1][2]
- **Plan for width and permits.** A 102-inch (8 ft 6 in) width limit is cited in the discussions, but a Cozy IV’s spar can be more than 13 feet wide. Check the rules and permits for every state on your route rather than assuming you can travel over-width. Tilting the fuselage was used to reduce width on some Cozy IV moves. [5][9][12]
- **Use a stable, padded support and secure the load.** Builders describe foam padding, blankets or towels, flat straps, and tie-downs; wheel blocks can help prevent movement on a wood-deck trailer. [1][2][3] Take care with the nose: one aircraft was damaged when its nose tip rested on a piece of lumber, so use a properly padded, well-supported cradle rather than letting the tip bear directly on a block. [3]
- **Think carefully about the transport position.** One experienced hauler recommends keeping the aircraft level, while other owners describe transporting with it tilted or on its wheels. A Cozy IV owner who transported one flat on its wheels warned that its width created serious road and aircraft-damage risks. The right arrangement depends on the airframe, trailer, route, and legal width—don’t treat a method that worked for another aircraft as automatically safe for yours. [11][12]
- **Choose the trailer for the actual load.** One Cozy III owner found an 8 ft 6 in by 14 ft trailer barely adequate; another source says a 20-foot trailer is more suitable for a Cozy IV. [1][11]

For a long trip, carefully measure the aircraft and trailer, verify the loaded width and route restrictions, and get help from someone experienced with Cozy transport and a suitable cradle.

## Answer

A Cozy can be moved on a trailer, but the main challenge is **supporting it safely and staying within width limits**. The approach depends a lot on whether it’s a Cozy III or a Cozy IV, and whether you’re moving a flying airplane or a project in pieces.

### Common approach
For a Cozy III or Long-EZ, builders have commonly removed the wings, canard and propeller, then carried the fuselage on a wood-deck trailer. Use padded, broad supports and flat straps; block the wheels fore and aft so the airplane can’t roll. Protect and secure the wings and canard separately. A 14-foot trailer has been described as barely adequate, so check the actual fuselage length and trailer layout before committing.

**Don’t let the nose rest directly on a 2×4 or 4×4.** A reported transport damaged a nose that was supported that way. Make a padded cradle that supports the fuselage at appropriate structural locations, and have an experienced Cozy builder or technical adviser check the support plan.

### Width and the Cozy IV
The usual maximum legal width is **102 inches (8 ft 6 in)**, but rules and permits vary by jurisdiction. A Cozy III may be manageable on a trailer depending on configuration; the Cozy IV’s spar and overall width can be much greater. One Mk IV owner reported a wingless width close to 137 inches. For a wide load, investigate permits and route restrictions before the trip. Tipping the airplane sideways or building a special lifting trailer can reduce width, but requires a properly engineered cradle or stand—not improvised blocking.

Some builders report moving aircraft wider than the limit without being stopped. That’s not a safe or legal plan: an incident, construction zone, bridge or narrow road can make an oversize load especially hazardous.

### Before towing
- Confirm the trailer’s capacity, deck length, tie-down points and tire condition, and check the tow vehicle’s ratings.
- Support the airplane at appropriate structural points; don’t rely on padding alone to prevent movement.
- Secure control surfaces and every loose component, and protect the finish from straps and road debris.
- Check weight distribution, lighting, clearance and the route, including construction restrictions.
- Stop soon after starting, then periodically, to check straps and supports.

For a long trip or a Cozy IV, get help from someone who has transported that model before. A purpose-built cradle or professional aircraft transporter may be safer than adapting an ordinary trailer.
