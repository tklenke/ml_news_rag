For Matco brakes, **reverse/pressure bleeding from the caliper up to the master-cylinder reservoir** is usually the easiest method. It pushes air in the direction it naturally wants to travel.

1. **Use the correct fluid.** Matco recommends MIL-H-5606 or a compatible fluid. Matco permits DOT 5 silicone fluid, but **not DOT 5.1, DOT 3, or DOT 4**. Don’t mix fluids; check the current instructions for your specific brake model.
2. **Protect the aircraft and keep the reservoir from overflowing.** Put a clear hose on the master-cylinder reservoir or arrange to catch fluid there. Have absorbent material handy—reverse bleeding can send fluid out of the reservoir.
3. **Use a clean, fluid-filled pressure bleeder or syringe** at the caliper bleeder. Keep the tool and hose free of air, open the bleeder, and push fluid slowly until it reaches the reservoir without bubbles. Close the bleeder before removing the hose.
4. **Keep the caliper and lines positioned to help air escape.** A near-vertical caliper and brake lines that rise continuously toward the master cylinder make bleeding easier. Matco caliper galleries can trap air if the caliper is mounted at an awkward angle; follow Matco’s guidance for your model rather than relying on a single “minimum angle.”
5. **Check the pedal and leaks.** Top up the reservoir, work the pedals, and confirm a firm pedal with no leaks. If the pedal remains soft, try bleeding again and inspect high spots in the lines for trapped air.

A few useful hints:

- Clear tubing helps you see bubbles. Move or gently tap the caliper and high points in the lines while bleeding.
- If you remove or rotate a caliper, keep a spacer between the pads so the pistons can’t extend too far.
- Vacuum bleeding can work, but bubbles drawn past the bleeder threads may look like air in the system. Reverse pressure bleeding is often less confusing.
- Use clean, dedicated bleeding equipment; don’t introduce moisture or contamination into the brake fluid.

The method most often recommended is a **reverse (uphill) pressure bleed**: force fluid in through the caliper bleeder and let it push air up the brake line and out at the master-cylinder reservoir. [2][5][9]

1. Use **MIL-H-5606 or a fluid Matco approves**. Matco’s cited guidance allows DOT 5, but says not to use DOT 5.1, DOT 3, or DOT 4. [1]
2. With the aircraft secure, connect a pressure pot and clear hose to the caliper bleeder. Use controlled pressure and watch the reservoir as fluid rises; arrange to catch or drain any overflow. [5][12]
3. Continue until the fluid coming up is free of bubbles, then close the bleeder and check the system for leaks and firm pedal feel. Clear tubing makes bubbles easier to spot. [10]

**Hints:** Keep the caliper close to vertical if its installation allows, and route the brake line so it rises toward the master cylinder as continuously as practical. Those details help air escape rather than collect in high spots. [14] Retract the pistons when bleeding, especially with new pads. [14] If air seems trapped, changing the caliper’s orientation and bleeding again may help; if necessary, isolate the caliper with a short line to troubleshoot it. [6] Verify the fluid and bleeding procedure against the instructions for your specific Matco setup before flying.
