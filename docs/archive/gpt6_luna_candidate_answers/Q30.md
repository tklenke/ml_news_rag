## Answer

Vortex generators (VGs) appear **potentially useful on a Cozy, but the evidence in these reports is mostly anecdotal—not a reliable promise of a particular landing-speed or distance reduction**.

- Cozy and other canard-aircraft owners report roughly **5–10 knots lower minimum speed** with VGs on the canard and main wing; one owner reported about **8 knots**. Other reports mention 10–12 knots, but the supplied material doesn’t include controlled before-and-after testing that verifies stall speed across configurations.
- A lower *actual* stall speed could allow a slower approach and less energy to dissipate on landing. But a slower-feeling approach by itself doesn’t prove stall speed has fallen, and the reports don’t establish a predictable Cozy landing-distance saving.
- There are tradeoffs: one Cozy-related report cites about an **8-knot cruise-speed penalty**. VGs can also change stall behavior, so the aircraft may feel different near the stall.

For a Cozy, treat the claimed reductions as a reason to investigate—not as a basis for changing approach speeds or assuming you’ll need less runway. Any installation should be evaluated with a careful flight-test plan, including stall behavior at relevant weights and CGs. Also, **vortilons**—the small leading-edge devices fitted to many canards—are not the same thing as VGs.
