## Answer

For Matco brakes, **reverse bleeding is often the easiest method**: push clean fluid in at the caliper and let the air rise out through the master cylinder reservoir.

1. **Confirm the fluid first.** Matco recommends MIL-H-5606 or compatible fluid; its guidance allows DOT 5 silicone fluid, but **not DOT 5.1, DOT 3, or DOT 4**. Don’t mix fluid types. If the system has had an incompatible fluid in it, get Matco’s instructions on flushing and seal replacement.
2. **Use clean, dedicated equipment**—a small pressure pot or syringe, clear tubing, and the correct fitting for the caliper bleeder. Keep the reservoir open and watch it closely so it doesn’t overflow.
3. **Push fluid slowly from the caliper bleeder upward** until bubble-free fluid reaches the reservoir. Avoid excessive pressure. Gently tapping the caliper and high points in the lines can help dislodge trapped bubbles.
4. **Close the bleeder before removing the hose**, clean up any spilled fluid, and check carefully for leaks. Then check pedal firmness and travel.

Caliper angle and internal passage layout affect how easily air escapes. Builders report different mounting angles, so I wouldn’t cut or reposition the strut based on a general rule; check the instructions for your exact Matco caliper, or ask Matco. If you use a vacuum bleeder, bubbles can sometimes be drawn past the bleeder threads and make it look as though air is still coming through.

If the brakes remain soft after a careful bleed, check for leaks, trapped high points, master-cylinder travel/rigging, and seal condition rather than continuing to pump indefinitely. Have the completed system inspected and verify braking on the ground before flight.
