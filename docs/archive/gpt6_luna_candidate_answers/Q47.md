## Answer

You can greatly reduce the risk of nosewheel shimmy, but no setup can guarantee it will never occur. On a COZY MKIV, focus on **caster geometry, damper friction, and keeping the nose gear in good condition**:

- **Check the steering-axis angle under load.** With the airplane loaded as it will be operated—especially at forward CG—the pivot should have the positive caster called for in the plans: the top of the steering axis aft of the bottom. Confirm it remains within the plans’ geometry when the strut is compressed; heavy braking or a compressed strut can move the geometry toward a shimmy-prone position. Have a COZY-experienced mechanic or builder verify it if you’re unsure.
- **Check damper breakout friction regularly.** With the nosewheel unloaded, use a fish scale at the axle to measure the force needed to start the fork turning. The supplied guidance gives roughly **2–4 lb** (some guidance says **3–5 lb**). Use the plans or applicable service guidance for your installation, and adjust only enough to provide the specified friction while keeping the wheel steerable. Recheck after maintenance and periodically in service.
- **Inspect for looseness, binding, or wear.** Check the NG6/pivot for side play; make sure the pivot moves smoothly and the NG17 tube is straight and not bent or ovalized. Also check the spring and extension, nosewheel tire balance and condition, and the main-wheel brake rotors. A weak spring, poor caster under compression, or a binding pivot can undermine the damper adjustment.
- **Use gentle ground handling.** On takeoff, rotate smoothly. On landing, keep the nosewheel off the runway as long as practical, then lower it gently at low speed; avoid a skip or bounce and avoid hard braking while the nose gear is heavily loaded. Keep the gear fully extended and locked as required for ground operations.

Shimmy can damage or break the fork very quickly and may endanger the propeller. If you experience shimmy, don’t treat it as a normal nuisance or simply tighten the damper: stop flying and inspect the entire nose-gear system before the next flight.
