## Answer

If you mean the **Cozy nose wheel**, there isn’t one universal axle-nut setting: it depends on the wheel and bearing assembly fitted. Check the wheel manufacturer’s instructions (often Matco) and the aircraft’s plans or builder records first. Don’t use a main-wheel procedure for the nose wheel unless it applies to that exact assembly.

For a nose wheel with **tapered roller bearings**, the general aim is to seat the bearings and leave the manufacturer-specified slight preload or endplay—not to clamp the wheel tightly. In broad terms:

1. Inspect the bearings, races, spacers, axle and nut; clean and grease the bearings as specified.
2. Assemble the wheel and spacers in the correct order.
3. While rotating the wheel to help seat the bearings, tighten the axle nut only as directed by the applicable instructions.
4. Check for axial or radial looseness and confirm the wheel turns freely. Seal drag can make this check misleading; use only the manufacturer’s approved method for dealing with seal drag.
5. Secure the nut with the specified locking method, and recheck the wheel for looseness and binding.

The material you provided describes turning the nut to a nearby cotter-pin position after seating the bearings, but that is **not a substitute for the instructions for your particular wheel**. Don’t assume a torque value, force the nut to the next slot, or shorten a spacer to remove wobble without the correct specification and qualified guidance. If you share the wheel/bearing make and model—or a photo of the assembly—I can help identify which instructions to look for. Because this is flight-critical, have an A&P/IA or appropriately qualified aircraft mechanic verify the setup before flight.
