## Answer

For a Cozy, the best technique is a **repeatable, airspeed-controlled procedure based on that particular aircraft’s POH/operating limits and flight-test data**. Amateur-built Cozys can differ in engine, propeller, weight, rigging, and handling, so don’t treat another Cozy’s numbers—or forum advice—as universal. Get checked out by an instructor experienced in canards.

### Takeoff
- **Plan conservatively:** verify weight and CG, runway length and condition, density altitude, wind, and the aircraft’s demonstrated takeoff performance. Use a long, smooth, hard runway while learning.
- Set takeoff trim and use the aircraft’s checklist. Apply power smoothly and maintain directional control with rudder; avoid riding a brake.
- As speed builds, use slight aft stick pressure to ease the load on the nosewheel. Rotate smoothly at the speed and attitude specified for your aircraft—don’t yank it off or raise the nose beyond the POH limit.
- Once airborne, establish the published climb attitude and speed. A short-ground-roll liftoff technique may not provide enough climb performance when heavy or at high density altitude.
- Decide in advance what conditions require an abort. If acceleration, directional control, or the prospect of reaching a safe climb speed is in doubt, reject the takeoff while there is runway remaining.
- Respect the aircraft’s crosswind limits. Strong crosswinds can require substantial braking and greatly increase the ground roll.

**Illustrative numbers only:** the supplied Rev. 05 Mark IV manual for one aircraft calls for a smooth rotation around **70 KIAS**, with an additional 5 knots for certain heavy/forward-CG conditions. That is not a general Cozy target; use your own aircraft’s approved procedure.

### Landing
- Fly a **stable, published approach** and control airspeed and altitude precisely. Don’t let the airplane get slow, high, or low while trying to salvage an approach; go around if it is not coming together.
- Use the landing configuration, speeds, and power technique specified for your Cozy. A canard Cozy generally uses much less flare than a conventional trainer: avoid an excessive nose-high flare, which can hide the runway and risk a prop strike.
- Aim for a controlled touchdown in the intended zone, then lower the nose as directed by the POH and maintain directional control. Don’t force a landing with excessive speed or let a slow, unstable approach develop.
- In a crosswind, use the method you have practiced and the aircraft’s guidance—commonly a wing-low correction or a crab with alignment before touchdown. Avoid locking a wheel with excessive rudder/braking.
- Practice first in benign conditions, with an experienced Cozy instructor and ample runway. If the approach is unstable, speed is wrong, or alignment is poor, **go around** rather than pressing on.

The supplied Mark IV manual gives example limits and procedures for its specific airplane; confirm every speed, limitation, and technique against the current documentation for the aircraft you’ll fly.
