## Answer

There isn’t a single standard throttle or mixture cable length for a Cozy with a Bendix RSA-5A servo. The right length depends on your throttle quadrant, servo position and orientation, cable supports, and the route you choose. A reported 96-inch throttle cable on one Cozy is only an example—not a reliable specification for another build.

**Measure the installed route:**

1. **Install or mock up the servo and cable supports** in their final positions. Decide where each cable housing will be clamped, including its support near the servo.
2. **Route a flexible wire or string along the intended cable path**, following the cable centerline from the control’s mounting point to the servo cable clamp. Include the actual bends and the chosen route around the tub, firewall, and engine. Don’t measure a straight line between the two ends.
3. **Check the route** against the cable maker’s minimum bend radius and clearance requirements. Avoid tight bends, chafing, heat, and unnecessary loops. A 45° or 90° turn may work if the cable permits it; don’t assume a 180° loop is needed.
4. **Confirm reach and travel at both ends.** With the control and servo lever at their specified stops, the cable must move the servo through its full range without bottoming out, stretching, or forcing a lever against a stop. Check the throttle and mixture independently.
5. **Order by the cable manufacturer’s measurement convention.** Cable “length” may mean housing length, overall length, or a dimension between specified end points. Confirm what they want measured, and specify the end fittings and required travel.

If possible, support the cable housing from the engine or servo so engine movement doesn’t tug on the control. Make sure the routing still allows for engine movement and that the cables operate freely throughout their range. Use end fittings appropriate for the servo levers and secure them with proper aircraft hardware; don’t rely on a questionable ball-end connection.

Before flight, have the installation and rigging checked by an appropriately qualified aircraft mechanic or your technical counselor.
