## Answer

For a Cozy, the best technique is a **controlled, stabilized takeoff and landing using the procedures and performance data for that particular aircraft**. Amateur-built aircraft can differ in weight, engine, propeller and handling, even within the same model. Get transition training from an instructor experienced in Cozy or similar canard aircraft.

### Takeoff

- Calculate takeoff distance and obstacle clearance for the actual weight, density altitude, runway slope and wind. Use a long runway while learning, and establish an abort point before starting.
- Set takeoff trim, apply power smoothly and maintain directional control. Crosswinds may require intermittent braking, which can substantially increase the ground roll; abort if a safe liftoff is in doubt.
- Rotate smoothly at the speed specified for your aircraft and conditions. **Do not raise the canard above the horizon**: excessive pitch risks a propeller strike.
- When heavy or at high density altitude, avoid lifting off at minimum speed merely to shorten the ground roll. The Cozy newsletter recommends ensuring you can reach an appropriate climb speed on the available runway; otherwise, reduce weight or wait for better conditions.

### Landing

- Fly a stabilized approach and hold the prescribed speeds accurately. One experienced EZ instructor uses **90 knots on downwind, 80 on base and 70 on final** as a training example—not as universal Cozy speeds. Use your aircraft’s tested speeds for its weight and conditions.
- Choose a long runway for initial practice. Canard aircraft need comparatively little flare, so expect a different sight picture and touchdown feel from a conventional trainer.
- Keep the runway in sight and don’t flare beyond the attitude that puts the canard on the horizon. If the approach is unstable, visibility over the nose is inadequate, or touchdown and stopping distance are in doubt, **go around**.
- In a crosswind, follow your aircraft’s procedures and limitations. Be especially careful not to apply full rudder in a way that locks a brake at touchdown.

The aircraft-specific owner’s manual, operating limitations and verified performance data take precedence over these general suggestions.
