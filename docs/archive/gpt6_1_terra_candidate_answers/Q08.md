## Answer

On a Cozy, the nose-wheel bearing adjustment depends on the **wheel and axle design**. Many use tapered roller bearings and spacers, but the exact procedure should come from the instructions for your particular installation.

The goal is **very slight bearing preload**: no perceptible side-to-side wobble, while the wheel turns freely. The rubber bearing seals can add drag, so drag alone doesn’t necessarily mean the bearings are too tight.

For a spacer-equipped nose wheel:

1. With the nose safely supported and the wheel off the ground, check for play by pushing and pulling the tire side to side.
2. Rotate the wheel while checking that the bearings seat and the wheel turns smoothly.
3. If the wheel still wobbles when the axle is correctly tightened, the internal spacers may be too long and preventing the bearings from being adjusted. Tightening the axle further won’t fix that.
4. Secure the axle hardware as specified for your installation, then recheck for play and smooth rotation.

One builder describes shortening an internal spacer in small increments to obtain slight preload, but **don’t machine a spacer based on a generic dimension**. Have an experienced aircraft mechanic or Cozy builder help verify the fit and the correct axle torque and locking method before flight.

Do you know the **wheel manufacturer and model**, and whether your nose axle uses a bolt and nut or a castellated nut with a cotter pin? That would help identify the right procedure.
