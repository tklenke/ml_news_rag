## Answer

You can **reduce the risk of nosewheel shimmy**, but no single adjustment guarantees it won’t happen. On a COZY MKIV, pay particular attention to damper friction, gear geometry under load, and the condition of the gear.

- **Check the friction damper before every flight.** With the nosewheel clear of the ground, use a spring scale at the axle bolt to measure the force needed to start the fork pivoting. One COZY builder source specifies **2–4 lb breakout force**. Another source gives 3–5 lb when pushing at the tire’s trailing edge; those measurements are taken at different points and shouldn’t be treated as interchangeable. Follow the applicable plans and the instructions for your particular installation.
- **Check caster with the aircraft loaded.** The steering pivot should be tilted aft at the top as specified by the plans. Strut compression under forward-CG loading or hard braking can change the angle and promote shimmy. Have an experienced COZY builder or mechanic check the geometry, gear extension, spring, and limit-switch setting—don’t adjust them experimentally.
- **Inspect the gear and wheel.** Look for pivot play or binding, a bent NG17 tube, a weak strut spring, tire or wheel imbalance, and brake problems. Don’t simply loosen the damper to mask a binding pivot.
- **Use smooth runway technique.** Rotate positively at the proper speed on takeoff; on landing, keep the nosewheel off until the speed is appropriately reduced, then lower it smoothly without allowing it to skip. Avoid unnecessarily hard braking, which can compress the strut and aggravate shimmy.

Shimmy can break the nosewheel fork very quickly. **If it occurs, have the gear, fork, wheel and surrounding structure inspected before the next flight**, and correct the cause rather than relying solely on more damper friction.
