## Answer

Owners’ sentiment is **open but cautious**: alternative engines can work well in a Cozy, but most discussions emphasize the extra engineering and testing compared with a conventional Lycoming installation.

- **Rotax has supporters.** One owner called the 916iS a solid modern choice, and its turbocharging is attractive for high-altitude operations. Rotax’s well-tested reduction drives also have a strong track record.
- **Installation is the main concern.** A lighter engine can create weight-and-balance challenges, and a pusher installation needs careful cooling design, especially on the ground. Propeller compatibility and the added complexity of a reduction drive also need evaluation.
- **Cost and effort can outweigh the benefits.** One prospective builder favored the conventional engine after considering weight-and-balance needs; another felt the Rotax’s performance benefits did not justify its higher price.
- **Other alternatives have succeeded.** A Cozy MKIV owner reported good experience with a UL520iS, while noting that departing from the plans took extra work. Automotive conversions are more controversial because they generally require substantial development.

In short, owners don’t dismiss alternatives, but they tend to favor a proven installation unless the builder is prepared for the added design work, cost and testing.
