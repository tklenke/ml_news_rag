## Answer

For Cozy Matco brakes, builders often recommend **reverse bleeding**: push fluid in at the caliper bleeder and up toward the master-cylinder reservoir, so the air travels upward with the fluid.

- Use a clean pressure bleeder or suitable pump and a clear hose at the caliper. Keep the pump supplied with fluid so it doesn’t introduce air. Watch the reservoir and prevent it from overflowing.
- Caliper orientation matters. For bottom-up bleeding, the bleeder is normally at the low point and the brake-line connection near the top. Air can still collect in internal passages or at high points in the lines. Clear tubing can help you spot bubbles, although it won’t show air trapped in metal components.
- **Don’t assume there’s a universal minimum mounting angle.** One W51LXT builder reported a mounting angle of about 10° but had trouble getting a complete bleed. Contact Matco about the internal porting and bleeding orientation for your exact caliper before changing its mounting or cutting the strut.
- Use the fluid specified for your components. The Matco guidance quoted by builders recommends MIL-H-5606 or compatible fluid; it says DOT 5 may be used, but **not DOT 3, DOT 4, or DOT 5.1**, which can damage seals. Confirm compatibility for the whole system and don’t mix fluids without following the manufacturer’s conversion procedure.

After bleeding, check for leaks, firm pedal feel and effective braking. If the pedal still travels excessively, don’t assume air is the only cause: check pedal geometry and master-cylinder travel as well. Matco specifies approximately **2.5:1 pedal mechanical advantage** in the supplied builder guidance.

Follow Matco’s instructions for your equipment, and have an experienced aircraft mechanic help if you’re unsure. Don’t fly with soft pedals, leaks or inadequate braking.
