## Answer

For a Cozy, a **front-hinged canopy (FHC)** may offer a safety advantage if a latch is missed, but it is a departure from the plans and puts more design responsibility on the builder.

| | Front-hinged | Plans side-hinged |
|---|---|---|
| **If unlatched in flight** | Often favored as a passive-safety improvement. Its behavior depends on the design; it is **not guaranteed to stay closed**. | Builders report serious accidents following unintended openings, particularly on takeoff. Proper latching remains essential. |
| **Access** | Generally easier access to the right front seat. Rear access depends on whether the canopy is full-length or split. | You can follow the established plans arrangement; access is affected by the canopy opening to one side. |
| **Construction** | Requires engineering and fabrication of hinges, structure, supports and latches. One builder estimated 75–100 extra hours, but another reported taking much longer. | Follows the plans, with less design work, though hinge alignment and fitting still need care. |
| **Weight and handling** | A full-length canopy can be heavy and awkward to lift or secure. Weight depends on the mechanism; one builder estimated about 2 lb extra. | Avoids the additional forward-hinge mechanism. |
| **Wind on the ground** | The open canopy can act like a sail. One FHC kit reportedly recommends not opening above 12 knots; limits are design-specific. | Also needs to be controlled and secured in wind. |

**A useful compromise is a split canopy:** front-hinged over the front seats, with a side-hinged rear section. Builders report that this makes the front section lighter and preserves good rear-seat and baggage access. The front section can overlap and capture the rear section when closed, reducing the risk of the rear section opening. It still requires careful design of the overlap, latches, seals and supporting structure. Gull-wing or removable rear sections are other options, but can compromise access or add work.

Whichever you choose, provide reliable latching and a positive pre-takeoff check. For an FHC—especially an electrically operated one—ensure occupants can release and open it after power or mechanism failure, and that rescuers can open it from outside. Have deviations from the plans reviewed by a qualified aircraft structures engineer; anecdotal builder experience is not structural validation.
