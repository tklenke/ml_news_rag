## Answer

Moving a Cozy by trailer is practical, but the **model and loaded width** matter. Builders have moved both Cozy IIIs and Mk IVs long distances on flatbed or car-hauler trailers.

- **Choose a suitable trailer.** One Cozy III owner found an 8 ft 6 in × 14 ft trailer barely adequate. Owners have used tandem-axle trailers around 16–20 ft for the Mk IV. Check the trailer’s capacity, tow vehicle rating and load balance.
- **Measure before loading.** A Cozy III owner reported a 9 ft 3 in main spar; Mk IV owners reported substantially greater widths. A U.S. general legal-width limit is often 102 inches, but rules, permits and route restrictions vary. Check every state and road on your route rather than relying on other owners’ experiences without permits.
- **Remove and protect components.** Owners typically remove the wings, canard and propeller. Secure the wings on the trailer bed with broad straps and generous foam padding; protect the canard and elevators as well.
- **Plan the support carefully.** Some owners use a purpose-built cradle to tilt the aircraft and reduce its road width. Loading and supporting it this way needs careful planning and help. One Mk IV owner strongly warned against hauling it flat at full width because of the danger to other traffic and the difficulty of passing through narrow construction zones.
- **Avoid point loads.** Use a padded cradle rather than resting the nose on a bare piece of lumber—one owner observed serious nose damage from that. Block the wheels if carrying it on its gear, and use suitable tie-downs at appropriate structural points. Check straps and supports during the trip.

An alternative mentioned for long trips is removing the gear and supporting the aircraft at the engine mount, but the support must be properly designed. After transport, have a qualified aircraft mechanic or experienced Cozy builder inspect the airframe and help with reassembly.
