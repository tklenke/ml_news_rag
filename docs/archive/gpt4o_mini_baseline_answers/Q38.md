The Wilhelmson electric nose gear actuator is a commonly used system in Cozy amateur-built aircraft, but it has been reported to have both common and uncommon failure modes. Let’s break down these issues based on feedback from users and previous discussions:

### Common Failures:

1. **Nose Gear Actuator Collapse**:
   - A frequent issue reported is the collapse of the nose gear during landing or taxiing. This is often attributed to the failure of the NG30s, which is known to occur every six months or so in certain installations. Users have noted that the original design of the NG30s may be inadequate for heavier front loads typical in Cozy aircraft. Reinforcement of the gear mechanism is often recommended to avoid these collapses.

2. **Loose Mounting Pins and Bolts**:
   - It has been noted that the mounting pins (such as AN4 12A bolts) that secure the gear mechanism to the airframe can loosen over time. This can result in catastrophic failures, as the mechanism risks detachment. Several users recommend using locktite, safety wiring, or regularly checking and tightening these bolts as a preventive measure.

3. **Flaky Relays and Faulty Switches**:
   - Relays in the wiring harness controlling the gear can fail, causing intermittent operation. Users have reported issues with the relay sticking, which can prevent the gear from cycling correctly. Regular inspection and, if necessary, replacement of these components is advisable.

4. **Misadjusted Limit Switches**:
   - If the microswitches that detect the position of the gear are misadjusted, they may fail to stop the actuator at the correct position, leading to potential gear damage or retraction while on the ground.

### Uncommon Failures:

1. **Quick and Catastrophic Failures**:
   - Some incidents involve the nose gear abruptly slamming down, which could indicate a sudden failure in the mechanical components, such as broken gears within the actuator. This type of failure is less common but can result in significant damage and safety risks.

2. **Gear Symptoms Related to Weight Distribution**:
   - There have been cases where the system failed under what should be normal operational conditions, possibly due to excessive nose weight. Such failures might not happen to all users, indicating a unique combination of installation and operating conditions leading to issues.

3. **Mechanical Wear Over Time**:
   - Components like the drive assembly can develop slop or wobble over years of use, indicating potential wear. Although this might not lead directly to a failure, it could eventually compromise the system's reliability.

### Troubleshooting Tips:

- **Regular Inspections**: Carry out routine checks of the actuator, its mounting pins, and attached bolts to ensure they are secure.
- **Check Electrical Components**: Regularly inspect the relays and wiring harness for signs of wear or failure.
- **Reinforce Weak Points**: If you are aware of any design weaknesses, such as the NG30s on your specific aircraft, consider reinforcing those areas based on community recommendations to prevent future failures.
- **Emergency Protocols**: Implement redundancy and emergency protocols in case of gear failure, ensuring you have backup systems in place.

By addressing these common and uncommon failure points and adhering to best practices for maintenance and operation, Cozy aircraft owners can help mitigate the risk of nose gear actuator failures.
